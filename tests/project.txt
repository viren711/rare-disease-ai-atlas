RARE DISEASE ATLAS
==================
Hackathon: 7th Global AI Hackathon, Challenge 05 (OpenAI x Buffalo Initiative x Hack-Nation)

THE PROBLEM
-----------
A family learns their child has a rare genetic disease with no approved treatment. They become the organiser
of a research effort they never expected to lead. The knowledge they need is scattered across papers, disease
databases, trial registries, grant databases and patient organisations. Groups rebuild registries and study
designs that another community already has, because diseases are filed by NAME, while research can be shared
by MECHANISM and SYMPTOM PATTERN.

THE SOLUTION
------------
One search box that opens a map of connected evidence. From a disease the atlas finds:
  - WHO SHARES OUR DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS   related diseases by symptoms, pathway and literature
  - WHAT USEFUL WORK ALREADY EXISTS          trials, registries, natural history studies, grants, variants
  - WHAT TO DO TOGETHER NEXT                 collaborators, patient groups, a sourced proposal, next steps
Every connection shows its source, date, confidence and any contradicting evidence. When nothing supported
exists, it says so, lists what was searched, and names the next question to test.

HOW IT WORKS (WORKFLOW)
-----------------------
1. COLLECT  Public sources, downloaded in parallel and resumable:
            MONDO, HPO, Orphanet, Reactome (biology) - PubMed + PubTator3 (26,026 abstracts) -
            ClinicalTrials.gov (1,303 studies) - NIH RePORTER (1,779 grants) - ClinVar (variants) -
            patient organisation list with checked websites.
2. RESOLVE  One stable ID per disease/gene/symptom (MONDO, HGNC, HPO). Synonyms map to the same node, so
            "globoid cell leukodystrophy", "GALC deficiency" and "Krabbe" all find the same disease.
3. CONNECT  ~61k nodes and ~207k edges. Each edge records source, date, confidence and an evidence type:
            database record (solid) / found in papers (dashed) / inferred by the atlas (dotted).
4. CLUSTER  Diseases are scored on shared symptoms (rare symptoms count more), shared pathways and papers
            that mention both, then grouped. Related diseases come from mechanism, not name.
5. CHECK    Each link gets a confidence breakdown and a stance (supported / mixed / weak / unsupported),
            with possible counter-evidence: stopped trials and why, retracted or negative papers,
            disagreeing databases.
6. ACT      The action plan chains the evidence between two diseases, lists shared assets, researchers who
            work on both, patient groups, funding and funding gaps, what differs and must be checked, a
            readiness level, next steps, and a proposal text to send to a partner.
7. EXPLAIN  A local language model rewrites the plan in plain language; every sentence keeps its citation and
            is verified against the facts, with a template fallback.
8. ASK      Natural-language questions over the abstracts get a cited answer, or a clear refusal when the
            literature does not support one.

DEMO JOURNEY
------------
Krabbe disease -> X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy (different disease families, shared neurological symptoms,
NO shared gene or pathway - the atlas says so) -> 17 shared trials/registries/natural history studies ->
over 100 researchers who published on both -> two leukodystrophy patient groups serving both -> readiness
"moderate" with the differences to check -> a sourced proposal. A sparse disease instead returns an honest
gap report.

WHAT MAKES IT TRUSTWORTHY
-------------------------
- Observations are separated from hypotheses (line style and labels everywhere).
- Every sentence in an explanation cites an edge; invented terms are rejected.
- Contradictions and gaps are shown beside the connection they affect.
- Off-topic or unsupported questions are refused, not guessed.

RUNS ANYWHERE
-------------
CPU-only laptop, no keys, no cloud: Streamlit UI + in-memory graph + local 4-bit Qwen2.5-3B.
Start with ./start. Light and dark themes.

LIMITS (HONEST)
---------------
Two disease groups only. Researcher identity is name-based without ORCID. Counter-evidence flags are
automated heuristics. Funding gaps cover NIH only. Leads are for researchers and doctors to check;
they are not medical advice.

10x CLAIM (to validate)
-----------------------
Milestone: launching a shared natural history study. Today a group builds one from scratch (typically years
of protocol design, registry setup and recruitment). With the atlas it finds an existing registry/study
design for a mechanistically similar disease, the researchers who ran it, the patient group that recruited,
and the differences to review, so the group adapts instead of rebuilding. Assumptions to test: the reused
design needs expert review for eligibility and endpoints; recruitment is the slowest step and is shortened
only if the partner community shares its network.
